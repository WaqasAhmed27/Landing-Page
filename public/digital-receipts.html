What Are Digital Receipts?

A digital receipt is an electronic record of a retail transaction delivered to a customer's smartphone instead of (or alongside) a printed thermal paper receipt. Digital receipts contain the same itemized information as paper receipts - products, quantities, prices, taxes, and totals - but are delivered instantly to the customer's mobile device without requiring an app, account, or phone number.

In Pakistan's retail sector, digital receipts are delivered primarily via two methods: NFC tap (the customer taps their phone on a counter device) and QR code scan (the customer scans a high-density QR displayed at checkout). Both methods open the receipt directly in the customer's native browser in under two seconds.

Why Email Receipts Fail at Checkout - And Why NFC/QR Wins

Email receipts require the customer to verbally provide their email address at checkout. This creates friction, slows checkout speed, and raises privacy concerns for customers. In Pakistan's retail environment - high volume, fast-paced, Urdu-speaking staff - email collection is impractical at scale.

NFC and QR receipts eliminate this friction completely. The customer taps or scans; the receipt appears. No data entry, no verbal exchange, no account creation. For high-volume retail formats (supermarkets, pharmacies, QSRs), this means zero checkout speed impact versus traditional paper receipts.

How Digital Receipts Enable FBR Fiscalization Compliance

The Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) requires Tier-1 retailers to electronically transmit fiscal invoice data for every point-of-sale transaction. TapTile intercepts the ESC/POS receipt data stream, automatically formats it into the required FBR invoice structure, generates a verifiable FBR QR code, and transmits it to FBR servers in real time - simultaneously delivering the digital receipt to the customer.

This means TapTile handles both customer receipt delivery and FBR fiscal compliance in a single hardware installation, with no additional integration required.
